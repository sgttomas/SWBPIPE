# Seismic component-weight references: the self-weight plan at the seismic case's gravity

Frozen, independently derived expectations for the static-equivalent seismic
rule of [DEL-05-01](../../../execution/PKG-05/DEL-05-01/ScopeOfWork.md) as
SWBPIPE's Agent 0 decided it. The generated seismic load is "the self-weight
plan evaluated at the seismic case's gravity, with each entered component
force W held fixed":

- **Every member:** for each global axis, the load per length is the g-factor
  times the seismic case's gravity times the member's mass per length.
- **A member whose component weight the self-weight plan applies:** in place
  of that load, the force is the g-factor times the component's weight W at
  the component's node, with the moments of that force about the node from the
  centre-of-gravity vector.
- **A member whose component weight the plan does not apply** keeps its
  distributed seismic load.

**This is a force convention.** The generated seismic loads follow the
self-weight plan evaluated at the seismic case's gravity, with each entered
component force W held fixed. It is not a statement that the seismic load uses
one fixed mass. Equal gravities are a sufficient condition for it to coincide
with acceleration of the model's self-weight mass: when the seismic case's
gravity equals the gravity of an applied self-weight, the two agree. When they
differ, the pipes' loads scale with the seismic case's gravity and the
component force does not. The two then still agree on a component whose
applied weight is zero: it contributes no difference at any gravity
(boundary 4). Where the applied
self-weight's gravity is absent or ambiguous, the equivalence of the two mass
models is not established, and this package gives the value the convention
yields without calling it equivalent.

The convention rests on its own merits. It needs no division by a gravity, no
knowledge of where the user's W came from (a weighed force, a catalogue
force, a mass times some gravity), and it refuses nothing.

A model-check warning is to be built with the engine change. It names an
exception: a seismic case that carries an applied component weight and states
a gravity different from the applied self-weight's, with both values. The
absence of the warning does not establish that one mass model holds. Each
seismic reference case here records whether the warning is expected.

The references were written before the engine change they will judge. No
product comparison binds them yet.

Nothing here is generated by the product. The generator imports, reads and runs
no product code. It reads only its package's files. All inputs are invented and
deliberately non-standard. No value or example from the `domains/piping-design/`
corpus or from any catalogue is used. **There is no internal pressure anywhere
in this package**: no pressure region and no pressure load. The seismic load
does not depend on pressure. The exact route's pressure bore enters only as
the contents' area. The product supplies no g-factor, gravity or weight: all
are the user's inputs here. The standard claim fence applies.

## Generated loads

Under a seismic case with gravity g and g-factors k = (k_x, k_y, k_z):

- **AB and CD** (unmapped, or mapped by the not-consumed flange) carry
  q = μ·g·k in N/m, uniform over the whole member.
- **BC** carries no distributed load. It carries q = μ·g·k when the valve is
  not consumed (boundary 2).
- **Node C** carries F = W_v·k and M = r_v × F. When the valve is not
  consumed, C carries nothing.

and C carries F = (0, 0, −W_v) with M = r_v × F. That is the self-weight plan
of DEL-05-01, with W a force that the size of the acceleration does not scale.

## Re-deriving by hand

**Statics (CANT).** The reaction at A is minus the resultant. Example,
CANT-PF SEISMIC-Y:

- R_Ay = −k_y(μg·(L_AB + L_CD) + W_v) = −0.35·6797.384 = −2379.08 N.
- M_Az = −k_y[μg(L_AB²/2 + L_CD(x_C + L_CD/2)) + W_v(x_C + r_vx)]
  = −0.35(1111.846·9.05 + 2350·2.675) = −5721.96 N·m.
- M_Ax = −(−r_vz·k_y·W_v) = 102.8125 N·m.

**Section actions.** At x, the section action is the resultant of everything
outboard of x, about (x, 0, 0), acting on the +x face of the inboard part:
N = F_x, V_y = F_y, V_z = F_z, T = M_x, and M_y and M_z with the lever
(x_n − x, 0, 0). The member end force at i is −S(x_i⁺) and at j is S(x_j⁻).

**Motions (unit-load virtual work).** Integrate from 0 to x_P over every
member, with EI, EA and GJ multiplied by 3 on BC wherever the valve is
applied:

- u_x = ∫N/EA
- θ_x = ∫T/GJ
- θ_z = ∫M_z/EI
- u_y = ∫M_z(x_P − x)/EI
- θ_y = ∫M_y/EI
- u_z = −∫M_y(x_P − x)/EI

A right-handed rotation about +Y turns +X toward −Z, which is the reason for
the sign of u_z.

**FIXED.** The axial, torsion, x–y and x–z problems decouple. Release D and
take its six actions as redundants. The CANT motions at D under the loads
(δ₀), with the 6×6 flexibility of unit actions at D (f), give the actions at D
from f·R_D = −δ₀. Then superpose. Axial and torsion have one redundant each;
each bending plane has two.

**The other method in the generator.** It also assembles the 24-DOF direct
stiffness system with work-consistent nodal loads, for a uniform q on length l:

- q_y: (ql/2, ql²/12) at i and (ql/2, −ql²/12) at j on (u_y, θ_z);
- q_z: −ql²/12 at i and +ql²/12 at j on θ_y.

It solves that system exactly, and every motion, reaction and end force of the
two methods is equal as an exact value.

The two methods are not wholly independent. Besides the inputs and the
generated loads they share:

- the geometry;
- the section and stiffness construction;
- the exact arithmetic;
- the linear solver, which forms the displacements in one method and the
  redundants in the other.

They differ in how the response is formed: element assembly in one, and
section-action statics with virtual-work integrals in the other.

## Parity (item 4)

For each seismic case, take the weight of the model under gravity g_wt along
+e_a: the total weight 𝒲 and its moment about the origin M_w,a. Here g_wt is
the applied self-weight's gravity: g_w, or g_w_bits in boundaries 1 and 3. The
weight is always the self-weight plan's, even beside a control. Then the
identities are:

- resultant force = Σ_a k_a·𝒲·e_a;
- resultant moment = Σ_a k_a·M_w,a.

Each case's `parity` block carries the expected values, the differences and
`holds`. Parity is an invariant, not a further solution method. It calls the
same generation and resultant functions on both sides. So it shows the
decided rule's identity with the weight case, but it does not independently
establish which constituents of mass are selected. The mass and load tables
above state that, for a reviewer to check by hand.

- **The parity holds exactly in all 22 reference cases whose seismic
  gravity equals the applied self-weight's.** These are X, Y, Z, XYZ and
  TWO-SEISMIC-XYZ on each model and route, and boundary 1.
- **It also holds in CTRL-K-TIMES-WEIGHT-CASE.** That control is k times the
  weight case by construction.
- **Row by row:** every response row of SEISMIC-Z equals −k_z times the same
  row of WEIGHT-NZ (66 rows on CANT, 72 on FIXED; `row_identity`).
- **The two-mapped references equal the one-mapped references exactly.**
  TWO-SEISMIC-XYZ equals SEISMIC-XYZ and TWO-WEIGHT-NZ equals WEIGHT-NZ. The
  not-consumed flange changes nothing, in weight or in seismic.
- **Every other control fails the parity**, keyed-on-mapped included. There,
  the weight the parity compares with is the self-weight rule's: AB keeps its
  pipe weight.

## Different gravities (item 6)

In SEISMIC-XYZ-G10 the seismic case states 10 m/s², and the applied
self-weight uses g_w = 9.79 m/s².

- **Expected, under the decided rule:** AB and CD carry μ·10·k, and the valve
  force is k·W_v = (705, 822.5, 940) N, with no division by any gravity. In a
  product this case also carries the model-check warning that names it, with
  both gravities (`expected_model_check_warning.expected` is true). No warning
  code is assumed.
- **Control CTRL-G-RESCALED (fixed-mass inertia).** The component force is
  k·W_v·g_s/g_w, the inertia of the mass W_v/g_w that the weight case fixes:
  (720.123, 840.143, 960.163) N. It differs from the expected value by
  k·W_v·(10/g_w − 1) = (14805/979, 34545/1958, 19740/979) ≈ (15.1226,
  17.6430, 20.1634) N in force and (0.214236, −52.0469, 45.3803) N·m in moment,
  on both routes and models.
- **Control CTRL-K-TIMES-WEIGHT-CASE.** Everything is generated at g_w: k
  times the existing weight case.

**What this means for parity.** When the gravities differ, the seismic
resultant is not k times the applied weight case's total. It differs by
exactly the pipes' share, k·(g_s − g_w)·μ·(the length carrying distributed
load) per axis, with that share's moment. The component's share is k·W in
both, unscaled. Every such case carries `pipes_share_force` and
`pipes_share_moment`, and the generator checks that they equal the parity gap.
In G10:

- PF: ≈ (28.6195, 33.3895, 38.1594) N, with moments (0, −86.3356, 75.5436) N·m;
- EX: ≈ (31.2381, 36.4444, 41.6508) N, with moments (0, −94.2349, 82.4555) N·m.

Parity holds exactly against the weight that the seismic case's own gravity
would give the model, μ·g_s·(L_AB + L_CD) + W_v, with W unscaled
(`at_the_seismic_case_gravity.holds`).

## Open readings

- **O-1, "the same gravity": closed.** SWBPIPE's Agent 0 decided the rule
  quoted at the top: the self-weight plan evaluated at the seismic case's
  gravity, with W held fixed. The first revision computed this reading. The
  other reading is now control CTRL-K-TIMES-WEIGHT-CASE.
- **O-2. When a component's weight is not applied.** DEL-03-05 states one
  condition explicitly: declared not consumed by the solve ("solves as its
  pipe, with its pipe weight"). That condition is the one modelled here.
  DEL-03-05 AC-005 also has a component that declares mechanics geometry only,
  or nothing, "and whose scale or weight is not applied", and it "states the
  first condition that was not met". Those other conditions are not listed in
  the text, and no case here models them. The rule it follows is the same: its
  body solves as its pipe, and its member keeps its distributed load.
- **O-3. Sense of the g-factor.** The text says "for each global axis" and

## Product binding

The test that compares the product with these references has not been
written, and its tolerance belongs to the integrator. The DEL-05-01 analytic
tolerance is 1e-9 relative, with a stated absolute floor. The boundary 3 gap
is about 1e-13 N against forces of about 1e3 N, far below that tolerance. It
is a statement of the rule, not a resolvable product comparison of resultants.
The product's warning on that case is the comparable outcome.

The models use these schema members:

- the component kinds `valve` and `flange`, with `rigid_pipe_ref`, `weight`
  and `center_of_gravity`;
- `solver_consumption`, `mechanics_geometry_only` for the valve and
  `not_solver_consumed` for the flange, and for the valve in boundary 2;
- `stiffness_scaling_user_value` 3 on the valve;
- the seismic inputs `gravity_acceleration` and `g_factor_x`, `g_factor_y`,
  `g_factor_z`;
- on EX, `corrosion_allowance` 1/400 m, `euler_bernoulli`, and no pressure
  regions.

**Re-freeze rules.** No value changes to match a product output. A
disagreement goes back to this reference's owner and an independent refuter.
A change of rule or convention is a re-freeze, with a recorded reason and a new
refutation.
