# SWBPIPE thesis

## From Design Intent to Defensible Engineering Work

**Why agent participation must become particular to an engineering practice**

[Read the thesis](THESIS.md).

The SWBPIPE thesis asks why an environment for piping design should connect
physical intent, analytical idealization, meaningful operations and inspectable
evidence before professional reliance. It explains the reasons for building
that environment, using selected implementation cases and their limits rather
than expanding the product specification.

The argument develops through model meaning, agent participation, numerical
integrity, open mechanics and particular engineering data, judgment throughout
design, and handoff into further professional work. It then examines what
Chirality can transfer to other applications, what remains host-specific and
why re-derivation from current requirements must preserve accumulated learning.

The [shared conjecture](appendix_d_framework_s.md) retains its formal argument
and prose. C names temporal dependence. The necessary meaning at issue is S's
recognition of the necessity of contingency; N is existential affirmation,
not modal necessity. G remains undefined. The practical argument does not
require assent to the conjecture or derive engineering mechanisms from it.

This is explanatory research under the owner's direction, not a new product
contract, permission or acceptance act. Current requirements remain in the
[PRD](../PRD.md) and its adopted companions. Standard claim fence applies
(F-PIP-2; claims taxonomy per DEC-081).

The source basis is repository revision
`a602024416ace9652f61bb2043889c307fd4e09d`. Evidence distinguishes product
commitments, inspected implementation, defined tests and reported Design
accounts. No product tests were rerun to author the thesis. The connected
App–SWBPIPE activity and transfer to another practitioner remain questions

The [Root thesis](../../../../docs/thesis/README.md) concerns the shared means
of development. The [App thesis](../../../chirality-app-v4/docs/thesis/README.md)
examines workflow authoring and agent participation within applications.
SWBPIPE is their particular engineering instantiation, whose domain claims
need their own grounds.
