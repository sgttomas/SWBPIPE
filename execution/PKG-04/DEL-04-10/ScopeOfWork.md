# Scope of Work — DEL-04-10 Response-spectrum analysis

## Purpose and Objective Traceability

This deliverable computes the seismic response of the linear piping model to uniform support motion by the response-spectrum method, on DEL-04-09's modal basis. An engineer enters the spectra and gets the peak displacements, element end forces and support reactions the earthquake is estimated to cause (or, under an absolute-sum rule, the sum of the magnitudes it combines), each as an unsigned magnitude. It takes every spectrum, damping value and method choice from the user, corrects for the mass the extracted modes do not represent, treats snubbers as restraints in the dynamic analysis only, and refuses by name what it does not admit.

It serves SOW-082 under OBJ-003 (`execution/_Decomposition/SOFTWARE_DECOMP.md`, PKG-04, row DEL-04-10), and PRD §12.9 and §7 item 15. In `docs/RELEASE_TARGET_v1.0.md` it is the owning deliverable for response spectrum: it supplies the response-spectrum analysis of the release target's reference system, solved on the exact route in the desktop with no admitted family refused (exit criterion 1), and its closed-form or hand-calculation references, frozen with an independent generator (exit criterion 2). It also sets the spectrum screening class of the release target's benchmark method. The required capability is user-entered spectra, modal and directional combination, a missing-mass correction, and snubbers as a dynamic-only restraint.

- **OUT-001** — The spectrum request: spectra, direction assignments, method choices and the missing-mass input (REQ-001).
- **OUT-002** — The modal responses, the modal and directional combinations and the missing-mass correction (REQ-002 to REQ-004).
- **OUT-003** — Snubbers as dynamic-only restraints (REQ-005).
- **OUT-004** — The spectrum results, their table, the analysis-run record and the separate readers (REQ-006).
- **OUT-005** — The admitted domain, the frozen references and screening tolerance, the desktop and report surfaces, and the claim boundary (REQ-007 to REQ-010).
- **OUT-006** — Independent (multi-level) support motion, at Should (REQ-011).

## Deliverable Definition — Ontology

### CLM-001 — The method

**Uniform support motion.** For support motion along global direction k, every rigid support moves with the ground, and the free DOFs are excited by p_k = (M R_k)_f, the free rows of the full mass times the rigid translation of all DOFs, consistent-mass coupling to the restrained DOFs included. The modal basis publishes its participation on this excitation (DEL-04-09/REQ-005).

**Modal response.** With pseudo-spectral acceleration S_k(f) entered by the user and scaled by the user's factor s_k, each mode i of the modal basis, with participation Γ_ik = φ_iᵀp_k / m_i, has the modal displacement u_ik = Γ_ik · s_k·S_k(f_i) / ω_i² · φ_i, relative to the supports. At that displacement, recovered at the mode's displacement extremum:
- element end forces and moments are (K_e − ω_i² M_e)·u_ik on the element, so the element's own inertia is included and end forces at a node balance the nodal inertia;
- reactions are (K_cf − ω_i² M_cf)·u_ik at rigid restraints, so mass at restrained DOFs and consistent-mass coupling load the supports, and −K_s·u_ik at each compliant support s.

Every modal quantity stays signed per mode and per direction until combination.

**Modal combination**, on each response quantity separately, with R_i the signed periodic value of mode i in one direction:
- SRSS: R = √(Σ R_i²).
- CQC: R = √(Σ_i Σ_j ρ_ij R_i R_j), with ρ_ij = 8ζ²(1 + r)r^{3/2} / ((1 − r²)² + 4ζ²r(1 + r)²), r = ω_j/ω_i and ζ the spectra's damping ratio.
- Grouping: modes in ascending order form groups in which each frequency is within 10 % of the group's lowest, and R = √(Σ R_i² + 2 Σ_groups Σ_{i<j in the group} |R_i R_j|).
- Ten percent: R = √(Σ R_i² + 2 Σ |R_i R_j|), the second sum over every pair i < j with (f_j − f_i)/f_i ≤ 0.1, whether or not the two modes fall in one group. It is a different rule from grouping.
- Absolute sum: R = Σ |R_i|, taken over the cluster sums where modes of a cluster were summed first (Repeated modes, below). It is an upper bound on every other rule's combination of the same magnitudes. It is not an estimate of the peak, and it is not a bound on the response of unequal-frequency modes whose contributions cancelled inside a cluster.

**Repeated modes.** For an exactly repeated eigenvalue, every M-orthogonal basis of its eigenspace gives the same signed sum of its modes' contributions to any response quantity, whatever each shape's normalization: the sum is the response of the excitation's projection on the eigenspace. No single mode's contribution has that property, and mode-by-mode SRSS and the rules that use per-mode magnitudes need not preserve it (CQC does at an exactly repeated eigenvalue, where its coefficient is 1). The modes of one cluster of the modal basis are therefore summed with their signs first, R_c = Σ_{i in the cluster} R_i, and every modal rule above is applied to the cluster sums. A cluster here is DEL-04-09's: adjacent computed eigenvalues chained by its relative cluster tolerance δ_c (DEL-04-09/REQ-002, REQ-003). It is a numerical convention, not a statement that the eigenvalues are equal, and it is not the 10 % closeness of the grouping and ten percent methods. Each mode keeps its own f_i and ω_i² for its spectral ordinate, its displacement and its recovery; the cluster's lowest frequency stands for the cluster only in the correlation coefficient, in the closeness tests of the grouping and ten percent methods and in the periodic or rigid classification. The independence from the basis is exact only under both premises, equal eigenvalues and M-orthogonal shapes. DEL-04-09 checks the modal basis's M-orthogonality to a tolerance, and a breach makes the modal set, and so the spectrum result, Sensitive (REQ-002). For a cluster whose modes have unequal frequencies, summing them first is an adopted numerical convention: such modes have different spectral ordinates and recovery factors, and no independence from the basis is claimed for it, exactly or to within the orthogonality tolerance. For close but distinct modes it can differ materially from applying the named rule mode by mode, SRSS included, however small δ_c is.

**Rigid response.** With a rigid frequency f_r, the modes of a cluster whose lowest frequency is below f_r are periodic; the modes of every other cluster, and every mode not extracted, are rigid. The rigid acceleration is a_r,k = s_k·S_k(f_r). The rigid response of direction k is the static response, on the dynamic stiffness, to F_k = a_r,k·(M R_k − Σ_periodic Γ_ik M φ̂_i) over all DOFs. Its element end forces are K_e u − F_e with F_e the element's own share of F_k, and its reactions are K_cf u − F_k,c at rigid restraints and −K_s·u at compliant supports, so that the reactions balance the total of F_k. It assumes that the modes classified as rigid respond in phase with the ground.

**Directional combination.** SRSS over the directions requested; 100-40-40: the largest of R_a + 0.4 R_b + 0.4 R_c over the permutations of the directional magnitudes; or the absolute sum of the directional magnitudes.

**Independent support motion.** For support group g and direction k, the influence vector ψ_gk is the static displacement of the free DOFs under unit motion along k of the group's restrained DOFs and of the bases of its compliant supports, with the other groups fixed; the sum over all groups is the uniform case. The excitation is p_gk = (M ψ̂_gk)_f.

Every combined response is an unsigned magnitude.

- **A period query is located exactly against represented operands.** On a period table, the comparison has two operands. The first is the query frequency f as represented: the binary64 value M1's modal result publishes, or the user enters. The second is each knot as represented: the authored abscissa as binary64, after normalization through the unit catalog. The exact period T = 1/f of that represented f is compared as a rational with every represented knot and with the range ends, before any interpolation. Rounding 1/f therefore decides neither whether a periodic mode or the rigid frequency is inside the tabulated range (REQ-001, Range) nor which interval it lies in. The exact comparison selects the interval and decides in or out of range, and nothing more. It does not make either operand exact: the modal frequency carries the modal solve's own error, and a knot is the binary64 nearest to what the user wrote. After location, the interpolation is binary64 arithmetic, and the product states its rounding and the error bound of each value it publishes (the next entry). The reasons: the represented f is the value the modal solve publishes; a rounded reciprocal can land on a knot that T does not equal, or overflow; and the frozen references compare T exactly (convention 2). A consequence: a period knot is reached only by a query whose represented period equals it exactly. A knot authored as 0.1 s is represented by a binary64 above 1/10, so a query represented as exactly 10 Hz has a period below that knot. At the first knot, such a query is refused as outside the range.
- **Each published ordinate is stated to 1e-9 relative, or the evaluation is refused.** Evaluating a spectrum at a query publishes three values: the interpolated ordinate, the pseudo-acceleration S (the ordinate times g for a g-multiple table) and the scaled s·S. Each is published only with a relative error bound of at most 1e-9, REQ-008's verification tolerance, measured against the exact interpolation of the represented table at the represented query, conditional on IEEE 754 round to nearest and the unchecked library-function assumption below. Interfaces and downstream accounts of these bounds retain that qualification. Otherwise the evaluation publishes nothing and is refused (`SPECTRUM_EVALUATION_ACCURACY_UNSUPPORTED`). That refusal is distinct from a query outside the tabulated range and from an invalid record.
  - **The bound is derived, not fitted.** No intermediate overflows or underflows: each is carried as a binary64 significand and an integer exponent. The two interpolation weights are each formed directly as a positive quotient, never as 1 − t, and a logarithmic ordinate is reached from the knot with the larger weight. Each operation's rounding therefore enters with its condition number. The bound adds the rounding of the published value to binary64: none in the normal range, and at most 2⁻¹⁰⁷⁵ absolutely below it.
  - **What the derivation rests on.** It assumes IEEE 754 binary64 arithmetic with round to nearest. It also assumes that the platform's logarithm and exponential functions (`ln`, `ln_1p`, `exp`) are each within 2⁻⁵⁰ relative of the exact value. That premise is assumed for the platforms the product runs on: Apple's libm on macOS (the release baseline), glibc on Linux and the Microsoft C runtime on Windows. The code does not check it, and platform documentation does not establish a universal guarantee of that constant. A maintained higher precision sweep of final results supplies evidence only for the configurations actually exercised and recorded by each local or hosted run; it is not a direct check of the primitive-function premise. If a supported platform violates the assumed constant, the premise and affected accuracy claims reopen even if the final-result sweep still passes.
  - **What it gives.** Under these premises the computational bound is a few units in the last place for a linear ordinate. For a logarithmic ordinate it grows with |z| ≤ |ln(y₁/y₀)|/2 and stays below 10⁻¹¹ for positive finite tables before final materialization. Publication still depends on the implemented argument and a finite positive binary64 output. Refusal means that this argument does not establish the required accuracy, not that accurate representation is impossible.   - **Damping.** All spectra in one request have the same damping ratio, or the request is refused (`SPECTRUM_DAMPING_MISMATCH`).

  - **Modal basis.** The spectrum solve uses a modal request (DEL-04-09) that includes dynamic-only restraints and, where DEL-04-09 requires one (a load/reference-state document or nonlinear supports), names the state case whose resolved state defines the modal model.
  - **Range.** A periodic mode or a rigid frequency outside a spectrum's tabulated range is refused by name; no spectrum is extrapolated.
  - **Extraction premise.** When the correction is applied, the extracted modal set reaches f_r (the lowest frequency of its highest completed cluster is at or above f_r, or it is the whole finite spectrum), or the request is refused (`SPECTRUM_EXTRACTION_BELOW_RIGID_FREQUENCY`).
  - **Recording.** Every spectrum result binds its request. An admitted spectrum result also records the request in the analysis-run record; an experimental document is not recorded as an analysis run (Experimental reach).

### Solution

- **REQ-002** — *Modal responses.* Each modal response is formed from the published modal basis by CLM-001, on DEL-04-09's participation over the excitation p_k, in both of its normalization cases, and recovered to displacements, rotations, element end forces and moments, and support reactions, with the inertia terms of CLM-001, on the model's route. Spectral ordinates are interpolated by the spectrum's stated rule. The modal basis's standing is carried: a Sensitive modal set gives a Sensitive spectrum result. Reactions of rigid restraints come from the full residual and forces of compliant supports (springs and finite-stiffness snubbers) from their own stiffness, −K_s·u. Damping is represented only through the spectra, and each mode's quantities are recovered at its displacement extremum; the velocity-proportional parts of forces and reactions are neglected, which the result discloses as an adopted approximation. No bound on the resulting force or reaction error is claimed.
- **REQ-003** — *Combinations.*
  - **Modal combination**, per direction and per response quantity: SRSS; CQC with the Der Kiureghian correlation coefficient at the spectra's damping ratio; the grouping method (Regulatory Guide 1.92 Rev. 1's Grouping method) for closely spaced modes, with its closeness rule stated in the result (CLM-001); the ten percent method, with its closeness rule stated in the result (CLM-001); or the absolute sum. Each is applied to each response quantity separately, never to a combined vector.
  - **Repeated modes.** The modes of one cluster of the modal basis (DEL-04-09's cluster, by its cluster tolerance δ_c; not the 10 % closeness of the grouping and ten percent methods) are summed with their signs first, and the modal rule is applied across clusters (CLM-001), so that a combined response does not depend on which M-orthogonal basis the modal solve returned for an exactly repeated eigenvalue; for a cluster of unequal frequencies it is an adopted convention (CLM-001). The result states each cluster so combined: its modes, their frequencies and the frequency that stands for it.
  - **Directional combination:** SRSS of the directional responses; 100-40-40, the largest of the three permutations; or their absolute sum.
  - Both rules are recorded with the result.
- **REQ-004** — *Missing-mass correction (rigid response).* When applied, the modes of each cluster whose lowest frequency is below f_r are periodic and the rest, extracted or not, are rigid (CLM-001); every mode of a periodic cluster enters the periodic sum that the rigid load leaves out, and no mode of a rigid cluster does. For each direction k, the inertia at the rigid acceleration of everything the periodic modes leave out, over all DOFs, a_r,k·(M R_k − Σ_periodic Γ_ik M φ̂_i), is solved statically on the dynamic stiffness and recovered with its inertia to displacements, end forces and reactions in equilibrium (CLM-001). Extracted rigid modes are never combined as periodic responses. The rigid response is combined with the periodic part by the user's rule before directional combination. The result states, per direction, the periodic modes' cumulative effective-mass fraction and the fraction of the total mass in that direction they do not represent, including mass that moves with the supports, and states f_r and the premise of the rigid response. Without the correction, all extracted modes are periodic and the omission is disclosed with the same fractions.

### Results and readers

- **REQ-006** — *Results and separate readers.*
  - **Publication.** Results are published as the spectrum result family `openpipestress.spectrum_result` under the profile `product_preview_spectrum_v1`, with fixed limitation text, and the semantic table `openpipestress.result_semantics/spectrum-1`, outside the `results.v0.3` envelope. How a result admitted through the headless command is recorded against a saved model state (DEL-14-02) is stated before that surface is admitted.
  - **Content.** Two distinct kinds of row, over one roster of response quantities (Roster, below). **Combined rows**, per direction and combined over directions: displacements and rotations, element end forces and moments, and support reactions, each an unsigned magnitude with its unit and frame, stating that it is an unsigned combined magnitude. **Contribution rows**: the signed per-mode, per-direction periodic values and the signed rigid response from which the combined rows were formed, each with its unit and frame, each stating that it is a signed contribution, not a peak response, and each periodic row naming its mode's cluster. Signs are kept so that every combination can be recomputed. Under uniform support motion (CLM-001), each response quantity has one combined record: for each requested direction, the periodic magnitude, the rigid magnitude when the correction is applied, and their combination; and one magnitude over the directions, with the governing direction under 100-40-40. It has one contribution per periodic mode and requested direction and, when the correction is applied, one rigid contribution per requested direction. Independent support motion (REQ-011) is not built; these counts and the compliant-support force below are stated for uniform motion only.
  - **Roster.** The response quantities are derived, before any recovery (REQ-007), from the built dynamic model bound to the modal result: its nodes, and the members, connectors, tie rods and supports of its dynamic stiffness. Every one is published as a combined record with its contributions; none is selected, sampled or left out. They are:
    - *Nodes.* For every node of the modal result's node census, restrained and massless nodes included, the three displacements and three rotations, in global axes, relative to the supports. A component restrained on a global axis is zero; an imposed displacement of the state case is not part of it.
    - *Straight members.* For every straight member of the dynamic stiffness, at each end, the three forces and three moments on the member, in global axes and again in member axes: x from end i to end j, y the part of the member's reference vector normal to x, normalized, and z = x × y, as the frame kernel forms them. The straight members are the pipes; the members that meet at a tee; a reducer's chord between its nodes as placed; the member of a valve, a flange or another rigid-family component; the members of a geometry-only bend; and an annotation-only expansion joint as its pipe. Such a component has no row beside its member's.
    - *Realized arcs.* For every realized arc, at each end, the three forces and three moments on the arc, in global axes and again in the arc tangent frame at that end. That frame is the static result's: the tangent frame in which it publishes the arc's end rows and from which it rotates them to global coordinates (DEL-04-01/REQ-018). It is taken from there and is not defined a second time here. Each row is the arc's total end action, its inertia included, one quantity; its elastic and inertia parts are not rows. Member axes are never an arc's frame: a row that states an arc end in member axes is refused by name (`SPECTRUM_ROW_INVALID`, naming the row and its frame).
    - *Connectors.* For every objective connector, at each end, the three forces and three moments on the connector, in global axes, the moments about its end node; and once for the connector, its six generalized forces in the connector's own axes (axial, lateral y, lateral z, torsion, bending y, bending z), work-conjugate to its relative displacements. They are the connector's alone, without its tie rods' forces. The span the connector replaces has no rows.
    - *Tie rods.* For every tie rod, one axial force, the same at both ends.
    - *Rigid constraints.* For every global component that the modal model rigidly restrains at a node, one reaction: the force or moment of that component, a moment being about that node, from the residual of CLM-001. The row belongs to the constraint and names every support that holds it: an anchor, a rigid restraint, a rigid snubber, an imposed-displacement support, and a nonlinear support in a restraining state that the modal basis states. A friction support holds only its tangential degree of freedom, and only when it sticks; its normal is another support's constraint with its own state, or a load given as a value, which is no constraint.
      - *Beside the static result.* The static result publishes a reaction under each support, and the static solve refuses a model in which two supports rigidly restrain the same degree of freedom (`SUPPORT_INPUT_INVALID`) or in which an active nonlinear support acts on a restrained one. Every static reaction row therefore has one support, and a node's static rows can be summed over its supports. A spectrum reaction row is per constraint. Where one support holds the constraint, the row is that support's reaction and corresponds to its static row. A constraint can be held by more than one support only through a dynamic-only restraint, a rigid snubber on a degree of freedom another support restrains, which no static solve contains. Where the modal model admits that, the equations give one reaction and no division of it: one row is published, none per support, and the row states that it is the reaction of the constraint, that the supports it names hold it together, and that it is not divided between them or to be added once per support.
    - *Compliant supports.* For every spring and finite-stiffness snubber that acts on global components, its own force or moment −K_s·u for each component it acts on, in global axes, a moment being about its node. A variable spring's row is that of its stiffness; its installed, cold and hot loads are not part of it.
    - *No row.* A component body has no row of its own: its inertia loads its node and is in the end forces and reactions around it. A support that neither restrains nor has stiffness in the modal model (a constant-effort support, an inactive one-way or lift-off support, an open gap, a friction support's tangential direction when it slides or its normal does not bear) has no row. The result names each such support and the reason.
    - *Not yet stated: a restraint along a skew direction.* The rows of a rigid restraint, a spring or a snubber that acts along one skew direction, and the node displacements beside it, are not stated here. They need the method stated first: CLM-001 and REQ-002 give the excitation, the rigid solve and the reactions for restraints on global components only, while DEL-04-09 reduces a skew restraint through a constraint basis. The unit that recovers a skew restraint writes that method statement (the excitation, the rigid solve, and the attribution of reactions through the constraint basis) into CLM-001 and REQ-002, with DEL-04-09's owner, and states these rows with it, under its own independent reading. REQ-005 and AC-003 still commit skew snubbers and are unchanged. The release target's reference system has no such restraint: all its supports are axis-aligned and carry no authored direction (`fixtures/reference_system/SPECIFICATION.md` §6).
  - **Census.** The result states what the roster was derived from: every node; every straight member and arc with its end nodes; every connector with its end nodes; every tie rod with its connector; every rigid constraint with its node, its component and the supports that hold it; every compliant support with its node and components; and every support with no row, with its reason. For each frame other than global axes it states the axes its rows use, as vectors in global axes.
  - **Frame and sign.** Every row states its frame, one of: global axes, member axes, arc tangent frame, connector axes, rod axis. These names are the spectrum result family's own and are not the static result's coordinate-system values, in which a straight member's axes and an arc's tangent frame share one value. Every signed row (a contribution or a stated cluster sum) states its sign convention. A combined row states its frame and that it is an unsigned combined magnitude, and carries no sign convention.
    - *Bound to the static result's rows.* Where the static result publishes a row of the same kind of quantity in the same physical frame (a node's displacement or rotation; a straight member's or an arc's end action in its local frame or in global coordinates; a connector's generalized force or end action; a tie rod's force, which is along the rod axis; a support's reaction component in global axes), the spectrum rows of that quantity are in that frame and each signed spectrum row has that static row's sign convention. The `spectrum-1` semantic table names the corresponding static row kind for each kind of spectrum row and how identity, end and component map to it; for a rigid constraint held by one support, that support's static reaction row. The static row's published convention governs; the description below restates it and is not a second definition. Only the convention is taken: where a static row's statement also gives a recovery law of the static solve (a reference displacement, a reference elongation or pretension, a pressure wall force), that law is no part of the spectrum row, which is the incremental dynamic quantity ("Dynamic response only").
    - *As the static result publishes them.* Positive rotations and moments follow the right-hand rule about their axis. A signed displacement or rotation is positive along its axis. A signed element end force or moment, in either frame and for every family, is the generalized force acting on the element at its end node; the internal section force at end i is its negative. A connector's generalized force is positive along its connector axes. A tie-rod force is positive in tension, its axis running from its attachment at end i to its attachment at end j. A signed reaction is the force or moment the support applies to the piping.
    - *Where the static result has no row.* A snubber is absent from every static solve (REQ-005) and so has no static row. Its frame and sign are defined here in full: as a holder of a rigid constraint or as a compliant support on global components, its row is the force it applies to the piping, in global axes, positive along the axis.
    - *Beside a static station or stress row.* The static result's station rows follow the j-side section action, and its stress rows are recovered from it; for a member or an arc, that action at end i is the negative of the end action. A spectrum result publishes neither. A signed spectrum end row therefore carries the same frame and sign convention as the static end row of the same member end, and differs in sign convention from that member's station and stress rows. A surface that shows a spectrum row beside a static station or stress row states each row's convention (REQ-009; a requirement on the results view, which follows DEL-07-05/REQ-010).
    - *Combined in its own frame.* Where a quantity is published in two frames, both rows are published, each combined in its own frame from rotated signed contributions: a signed contribution is rotated into its row's frame before it is combined, and a combined magnitude belongs to the frame it was combined in. A combined magnitude is never rotated: no combined row is formed by rotating, projecting or scaling another combined row.

It excludes that state's own forces, pressure thrust, preload and reference loads: it is not the force present in the piping at that state, nor that force with the response added, and it is not a stress.

A rigid constraint is identified by its node's identity and component, and carries the identities of the supports that hold it. A mode and a cluster are identified as the modal result identifies them. A model identity is any non-blank string (DEL-02-01), so a key composed from these fields is a label only: no reader derives an identity by parsing one. For a cluster of one mode the contribution is the sum and is not repeated.

## Production and Verification Method — Praxeology

**Method choices that define the product.**

- *One modal basis.* The spectrum solve reads a published modal result and the modal mass model. It forms no mass and solves no eigenproblem of its own.
- *A cluster's modes are summed on the published participation factors.* The alternative is to form a cluster's modal coordinates η from the cluster's own mass Gram matrix, (Φ_cᵀ M Φ_c) η = Φ_cᵀ p_k, which is exact for any basis of an eigenspace, M-orthogonal or not. It is not adopted: it departs from the participation factors the modal result publishes, and the readers could not recompute it from that result. It is taken up again if measurement shows that M-orthogonality inside clusters limits agreement with the references at the verification tolerance.
- *Signed until combined.* Per-mode and rigid contributions are kept signed and published, so every combined magnitude can be recomputed from them.
- *The rigid response is one static solve per direction* on the dynamic stiffness; extracted rigid modes enter it with their signs.
- *References first.* The references and the screening class exist before any comparison run.
- *Readers do not re-solve.* The product reader and the test verifier recompute combined magnitudes from published contributions.

**How each criterion is examined.**

- **VER-001** — Executable check: observes each request refusal, and the absence of any default, at the product entry, the headless runner and the desktop.
- **VER-002** — Comparison with a named reference: the product against the frozen references in `validation/references/f2_spectrum`, at the verification tolerance, with a mutation of each method choice and repeat-run byte identity.
