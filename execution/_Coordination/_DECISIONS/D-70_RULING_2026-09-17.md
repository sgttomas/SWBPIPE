## Owner act

Ryan Tufts put the design session's sequencing recommendation to the piping session (Agent 0 of the run `../AgentRuns/HELP-HUMAN-PIPING-20260915-PRODUCTION-UI/`) and relayed its response:

> I put your recommendations to the Piping agent and this is their response.  Can we agree on this path forward?

Relayed piping-session text, verbatim except that one link target has been replaced: the original pointed at an absolute path inside the piping session's local worktree, and this record substitutes the equivalent run-relative path.

> I agree with the overall direction: capture a bounded rendering baseline, then let the human-focused redesign proceed. Their clarification makes that baseline worthwhile, while confirming that the redesigned product will need fresh performance acceptance.
> I would make three corrections to their recommendations:
>
> * Main-frame work should not become the sole responsiveness gate. It is a useful regression measure, but short CPU work does not establish timely GPU completion or visible feedback. That matters particularly with their larger canvas, richer geometry and annotations. Chrome exposes GPU activity and frame presentation separately for this reason. [Chrome performance reference](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools/performance/reference#frames)
> * A 60 Hz display is not inherently the wrong reference. V79 identifies a contribution from Chromium’s future-display estimate; it does not prove zero product influence or establish physical display timing. It expressly cautions against choosing 120 Hz merely to obtain a pass. [V79 findings](../AgentRuns/HELP-HUMAN-PIPING-20260915-PRODUCTION-UI/instances/GPU_ORBIT_BOUNDARY_DIAGNOSIS_V79/REPORT.md)
> * We need fresh runs at both sizes. The earlier passes belong to an earlier candidate. A baseline on the merged product requires five fresh runs each at 1,000 and 10,000 pipes—not simply filling three missing runs.
>
> My recommended agreement is:
>
> 1. Treat this next tranche as baseline characterization. Record main-frame work, available GPU evidence, interaction latency and reported presentation separately. Keep the original targets and failures visible. Preserve the settled-frame and resource requirements. This gives the redesign useful evidence without inventing a CPU-only replacement for product performance acceptance.
> 2. Make only the necessary benchmark adaptations before freezing the run. Use stable commands and typed identities for setup and queries. Retain real pointer and keyboard input when measuring interaction responsiveness; calling a selection handler directly would bypass part of what we need to measure.
> 3. Attempt one fresh cohort, then hand over the result. No further compositor investigation or automatic repair series. A capture failure or missed target remains an explicit limitation, but does not indefinitely hold redesign implementation.
> 4. Keep additional workloads separate. Both display modes and labels already have coverage. Add supported overlay/deformation observations using the redesign team’s rendering brief. Future fittings and annotation systems cannot be characterized accurately before they exist.
> 5. Reserve product UI changes for the redesign. No viewport, shell, results-panel or diagnostics-panel refactoring in this baseline tranche.
>
> Non-UI engineering can follow separately, but pressure, connectors and sparse execution should not automatically be treated as independent write scopes. Their solver/result contracts need coordination, and builds must stay clear of timed benchmark runs.
> I recommend making the baseline report—not a mandatory green orbit score—the handoff condition for starting the redesign. The original unresolved performance obligation would remain explicit and be addressed against the redesigned product.

The design session agreed to the five points and accepted all three corrections: main-frame work is a regression measure and not the responsiveness gate; a 60 Hz reference is legitimate when the target and the display are declared together; and the merged product needs five fresh runs at each size. It added four non-blocking items: record the canvas CSS dimensions, device pixel ratio and rendered label count with every run; treat overlay and deformation observations as a separate small run keyed to the design program's rendering brief, which follows the concept phase; have the design program propose the redesigned product's acceptance targets fresh for a later ruling; and keep non-UI workstreams out of `apps/desktop/src` and the benchmark harness, serialized against timed runs. The owner then ruled:

> I accept that certain past decisions will need to be revised in order to take this course and remain consistent with the direction provided.

## Adopted bounded effect

1. **The next piping tranche is baseline characterization** of the merged product at `8468a33c86adb622b25e98f98b0eaf28c7e9fa0e`, using the frozen 1,000- and 10,000-pipe fixtures, five fresh production-build runs per size, both display modes and labels on. Main-frame work, available GPU evidence, interaction latency and Chromium-reported presentation are recorded separately, together with the canvas CSS dimensions, device pixel ratio and rendered label count of each run. The original 2,000/100/200/16.7/33.3 ms targets and every failed run stay visible. The settled-frame and owned-resource obligations are preserved.
2. **Benchmark adaptations are limited to necessity.** Stable named commands and typed entity identities serve setup and queries. Real pointer and keyboard input remains the stimulus wherever interaction responsiveness is the subject of measurement.
3. **One fresh cohort attempt, then handover.** No further compositor investigation and no automatic repair series. A capture failure or a missed target is recorded as an explicit limitation and does not hold redesign implementation.
4. **Additional workloads stay separate.** Overlay and deformation observations are added as a separate small run keyed to the design program's rendering brief once that brief exists. Fittings and annotation systems that do not yet exist are not characterized in advance.
5. **No product UI changes in the baseline tranche**: no viewport, shell, results-panel or diagnostics-panel refactoring.
6. **The handoff condition for starting redesign implementation is the baseline report**, not a passing orbit score. The D-68 performance obligation remains explicit and is addressed against the redesigned product, under acceptance targets that are proposed with a score-independent rationale, frozen prospectively, applied identically to baseline and candidate, and ruled by the owner before use, consistent with V79's guidance.
7. **Non-UI engineering** (pressure runtime, connector mechanics, sparse execution) proceeds through coordinated solver and result contracts, is serialized against builds and timed benchmark runs, and touches neither `apps/desktop/src` nor the benchmark harness.
8. **The human-focused UI design program proceeds in parallel at the design and specification level** (research, concept directions, design system, mock drafts, UX specification) with no product implementation. It owes the piping session a rendering brief and owes the owner a proposal for the redesigned product's acceptance criteria.
