## 3. Reasoned-Selection Contract

### 3.1 Fast-reject boundary

A matter remains owner-gated when it cannot be responsibly grounded in the
accepted project, including when it depends on or performs:

1. irreducible personal preference or identity;
2. material project-purpose or accepted-scope inclusion/exclusion/boundary
   change;
3. new normative content or new/changed acceptance criteria;
4. professional, safety, legal, fiduciary, contractual, hazard, residual-risk,
   code-compliance, or regulatory accountability;
5. spending, procurement, licensing, third-party consent, or another external
   commitment;
6. publication, public release, issuance, release/reliance acceptance, or
   professional authentication;
7. protected/private/proprietary-data exposure;
8. destructive, irreversible, history-rewriting, or accepted-baseline merge
   action;
9. lifecycle/stage advancement, reproduction/prover/professional acceptance,
   evidence-posture promotion, accountable-party assignment, protected
   domain-engine/prover activation, or any other applicable D-52/D-49 limit;
10. unavailable/stale evidence, unresolved authority conflict, or a claim
    stronger than its warrant.

Ambiguity about whether a fast-reject boundary is touched remains a hit.
Several viable or defensible alternatives surviving is not itself a hit.

### 3.2 Eligibility and selection

After the fast-reject screen passes, the agent must:

1. ground every outcome-determining premise in live accepted project
   artifacts;
2. apply existing authority, purpose, scope, criteria, and values without
   creating any;
3. analyze ontology, epistemology, praxeology, and axiology with no omitted or
   balanced-away lens;
4. identify materially important viable alternatives and their tradeoffs;
5. exercise bounded discretion to select and advance the one project-grounded
   outcome it judges most consistent and coherent across the four lenses;
6. keep the immediate effect local, bounded, reviewable, and reversible before
   every nondelegable gate;
7. record a concise rationale naming the selected outcome and materially
   important rejected alternatives; and
8. obtain fresh independent refutation before effect.

Another defensible option does not by itself require referral. Referral or
`HOLD` remains correct when no responsible project-grounded judgment can be
made, the evidence is insufficient, or a fast-reject boundary is touched.
