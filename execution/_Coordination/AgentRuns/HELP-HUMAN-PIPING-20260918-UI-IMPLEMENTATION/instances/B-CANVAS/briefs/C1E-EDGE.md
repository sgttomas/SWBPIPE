### The mechanism: a band inside the figure material (P2's mechanism A), exact for a prism

ROOT's order of mechanisms is A, then B (an inverted hull), then C (analytic line quads); A and B may be combined if that serves; D is not tried. **You build A. If A does not hold, you stop and return the numbers; you do not start B.** Your parent decides the follow-on.

The manager's analysis, for you to verify and improve, not to take on trust:

1. *Why a band from `facing` alone is not enough.* The tube is a ten-sided prism with smooth normals. Its rasterised silhouette is a polygon vertex line, where the interpolated normal can still face the viewer by up to sin 18° ≈ 0.31. A band "where `facing` is small", or the distance estimate `facing / |∇facing|`, therefore thins or disappears at some orbit angles once a tube is more than a few tens of pixels wide, and the line pulses as the view turns. P2 named this weakness. It is avoidable.
2. *The exact form.* For a prism the silhouette seen from the camera is exactly two vertex lines, the two tangent vertices of the cross-section polygon as seen from the camera's position in the tube's own cross-section plane. Take, for each of the two, the plane through the camera and that vertex line. A function that is affine in the tube's local coordinates and zero on that plane (`g = dot(p.xz − v, n)`, signed so that the tube is on the positive side; the polygon lies wholly on one side because the plane is a supporting plane) has three useful properties: it is affine, so as a varying it interpolates exactly across every facet; it is zero exactly on the rasterised silhouette, because a plane through the camera projects to a line; and its screen-space derivatives give the distance to that line in device pixels, `d = g / length(vec2(dFdx(g), dFdy(g)))`, to first order, which is ample for a band one or two pixels wide. The band is `1 − smoothstep(w − 0.5, w + 0.5, min(d₊, d₋))` with `w` the line's width in device pixels; multisampling already softens the outer side.
3. *What the vertex shader needs.* The camera's position in the instance's local frame, and from it the two tangent vertices. With `M = modelViewMatrix * instanceMatrix` the camera is the origin of view space, the instance's columns are orthogonal (`compose` of a rotation and a scale; check that no layer above the meshes carries a scale or a shear, and say what you found), so the local coordinates of the camera are `dot(−M[3].xyz, M[i].xyz) / dot(M[i].xyz, M[i].xyz)` with no matrix inverse. In the cross-section plane the camera is at distance ρ (in radii) and angle α; side face k (centre angle `(k + ½)·2π/N`) faces the camera when `ρ·cos(α − centre) > cos(π/N)`; the two tangent vertices bound the run of facing faces. All of it is constant per instance, so every vertex of an instance computes the same two planes, and two varyings (one `vec2`) carry `g₊` and `g₋`. Guard the zero-scale matrix of a hidden instance (a division by zero there must not produce a warning or a visible fragment) and the camera inside a tube (ρ ≤ 1: no silhouette; draw no band). The manager checked two things numerically outside the product, and you may repeat them: with `β = acos(cos(π/N)/ρ)` and `Δ = 2π/N`, the tangent vertices are at `θ₊ = (floor((α + β − Δ/2)/Δ) + 1)·Δ` and `θ₋ = ceil((α − β − Δ/2)/Δ)·Δ`, and over 20,000 random camera positions from just outside the tube to thousands of radii away, for N of 8, 10 and 12, the line from the camera through each was a supporting line of the polygon every time; and under a 42° perspective at 2880 × 1656 device pixels, the derivative rule's distance differed from the true distance to the projected silhouette line by under 0.01 px on average, and by 0.34 px at worst, within 2 px of the line.
4. *Caps carry the ring for free.* A capped tube shows its cap wherever an end is open to view: at a free end, at a change of diameter seen from the smaller side, and as a sliver at an angled joint. On a cap fragment (`|normal.y|` near 1 in local space) the distance to the cap's polygon outline, in device pixels by the same derivative rule, gives a ring with no flag and no new data. Take every derivative before any branch: derivatives inside non-uniform control flow are undefined.
5. *What the caps cannot give.* The rim of a larger tube's end seen from the larger side, and a free end seen from behind: there the cap faces away and the line would have to be drawn on the side surface at the tube's end (`0.5 − |p.y|`, by the same rule). That needs to know, per pipe end, whether the end is free or meets a different section; drawing it at every end would put a ring at every joint between like tubes. That is stage 3.
6. *Geometry kinds.* The same prism code serves the branch and expansion-joint placeholders with their own side counts and baked radii (as material `defines`, so each kind compiles its own constants). The rigid box and the bend torus need another rule; for a box, the distance to the face's border from its `uv` by the same derivative rule outlines every visible face; for the torus choose the cheapest means that looks right at the close view, and say what you chose and what it costs. Placeholders are few and are replaced by real fitting geometry in a later tranche: adequate is enough, and a second mesh (mechanism B) for them alone is allowed if it is the cheaper route, with the ledger counting what it shares once.
7. *Width and colour.* `w` is the renderer's pixel ratio (1 CSS px), a uniform set where the theme's uniforms are set, not a constant and not read per frame; the line's colour is a uniform painted from `canvas.edge` by the same repaint path that paints `shadeRatio`, in the working colour space the `tint` uniform uses, so that the drawn pixel is the token. A figure material built without the line has neither uniform's code in its shader.

Tubes thinner than two line widths draw as the line's colour alone. That is what an outline does at a distance, and it is a named semantic change: at the fitted camera a large model's tubes read darker in light and lighter in dark than today.
