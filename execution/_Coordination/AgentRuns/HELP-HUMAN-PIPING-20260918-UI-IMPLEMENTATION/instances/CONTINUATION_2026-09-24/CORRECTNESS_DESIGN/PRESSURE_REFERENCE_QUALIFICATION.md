## 1. Independently checked mechanics

Take a sufficiently long straight circular tube, reference inner/outer radii `ri < ro`, constant uniform internal pressure increment `p`, zero external pressure increment, homogeneous isotropic elasticity `(E, nu)`, small strain, and section-uniform thermal strain `aT = alpha DeltaT`. Use tension-positive material stress. The boundary tractions are `sigma_r(ri)=-p`, `sigma_r(ro)=0`. These assumptions concern the regular tube away from end caps, local attachments and section discontinuities.

The compatible axisymmetric elastic stress family is `sigma_r=C-D/r^2`, `sigma_h=C+D/r^2`. It satisfies radial equilibrium `d(sigma_r)/dr+(sigma_r-sigma_h)/r=0`; the radial strain and circumferential strain from isotropic compliance integrate to a displacement of the form `u(r)=c1 r+c2/r`. Solving the two traction conditions gives

```
C = p ri^2/(ro^2-ri^2) = P/As
D = p ri^2 ro^2/(ro^2-ri^2)
Ai = pi ri^2; As = pi(ro^2-ri^2); P = p Ai
sigma_r + sigma_h = 2 P/As.
```

Axial isotropic compliance is `epsilon_z-aT=[sigma_z-nu(sigma_r+sigma_h)]/E`. Consequently the uniform axial membrane stress and its material-wall resultant are

```
sigma_z = E(epsilon_z-aT) + 2 nu P/As
Nw = As sigma_z = E As(epsilon_z-aT) + 2 nu P
S = Nw-P.
```

`P` and `E As` have dimensions of force; strain and `nu` are dimensionless. All stress terms have pressure dimensions. The positive Poisson term in `Nw` is required: pressure expands the circumference; preventing the corresponding axial contraction requires wall tension. The free *wall without axial cap transfer* therefore contracts for positive `nu,p`. A free closed tube with transferring closures instead carries `Nw=P` and extends by `(1-2nu)P/(E As)` per unit length. These are different boundary-value problems, not conflicting pressure theories.

`S` is the combined wall/fluid effective resultant, not material-wall force or an extra material stress. Dividing it by `As` would not give the axial membrane stress. Current Orcina theory distinguishes these resultants and supplies the same pressure/Poisson structure; COMSOL 6.4 describes the homogeneous axial Poisson strain and its initial-strain treatment. The equations above were derived from equilibrium and compliance, not copied from a commercial solver formula or inferred from agreement with production. [Orcina tension theory](https://www.orcina.com/webhelp/OrcaFlex/Content/html/Linetheory,Calculationstage1tensionforces.htm), [COMSOL pressure effects](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.sme/sme_ug_theory.06.165.html).

For local i-to-j axial displacement, set `epsilon0=aT-2nuP/(E As)`. The element's constitutive wall action is `qwall=K d-feigen`, where `feigen=[-E As epsilon0,+E As epsilon0]`. Mechanical distributed-load equivalents add their own subtraction in recovered endpoint actions. A transferring physical closure supplies the external pair `fcap=[-P,+P]`. Thus `qwall=K d-fmech-feigen`, with no second cap subtraction. For the free transferring case this recovers `qwall=fcap=[-P,+P]`, hence wall tension `P`; subtracting caps would incorrectly publish zero wall stress. Adding a separate longitudinal pressure stress to `Nw/As` would count the cap contribution twice.

Under fixed-end, pressure-only conditions `qwall=[-2nuP,+2nuP]`. Subtracting applied cap transfer from the assembled equilibrium residual gives support-on-pipe actions `[(1-2nu)P,-(1-2nu)P]`. This agrees with the declared support sign. The reaction calculation and wall recovery are different operations; the cap remains in the applied-load ledger for the former.

## 3. Closure and bend load-path qualifications

1. **Separate closures have zero net cap transfer to the modeled wall by definition.** They must actually be externally supported or compensated so the pressure-boundary force is carried elsewhere. This is not a generic “open pipe” with a magically sustained pressure, and is not established merely by naming an expansion joint. Remote closure support loads stay outside the pipe reaction ledger but remain explicitly disclosed. A tied/pressure-balanced assembly must be checked from its actual connections and effective areas; its connector stiffness and attachment forces cannot be inferred from this terminal label.
2. **Separate closures do not guarantee zero wall force under restraints.** `Nw=0` applies to the free wall example. Holding both ends at zero axial strain with separate closures still produces `Nw=2nuP` and pipe reactions `[-2nuP,+2nuP]` in the pressure-only case. This additional state is a useful independent discrimination case for runtime reference preparation.
3. **Mixed closure transfer needs global equilibrium.** One transmitting and one separate terminal creates a nonzero resultant applied wall cap load. A specified axial restraint can balance it. An unsupported free assembly cannot be rescued by inventing a second cap or suppressing the residual. Interior mathematical caps cancel only for the common bore/pressure and correct global direction assumed by this first contract.
4. **A welded bend does have a fluid pressure force on its wetted wall.** For a uniform static-pressure, equal-bore bend with incoming/outgoing tangents `tin,tout`, the fluid-on-wall resultant is `P(tin-tout)`. Adjacent wall cuts contribute `-Nw tin+Nw tout`. Their axial-resultant sum is therefore `(Nw-P)(tout-tin)=S(tout-tin)`. This elementary free-body illustration shows why omitting adjacent wall cuts falsely turns a pressure force into an additional external restraint demand. It is not a complete elbow constitutive model: shear, moments, local shell stresses, deformation and boundary compatibility remain.
5. **Neither zero pressure response nor universal extra bend thrust follows.** Closed-wall pressure forces can balance while pressure-induced extension/bend deformation produces restraint reactions. A consistent wall/cap formulation can represent the effects explicitly; an effective-resultant formulation can eliminate matching terms. Adding or deleting only a bend term without reconciling both changes the model. Abaqus documents pressure end-load cancellation and compensation at open ends, which independently supports this bookkeeping distinction. [Abaqus 2025 distributed loads, pressure on pipe and elbow elements](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAEPRCRefMap/simaprc-c-loaddistributed.htm).
6. **Static pressure is not every steady-flow load.** Even steady flow around a bend changes fluid momentum. Momentum flux, pressure drops, transient loads and fluid-structure interaction require their own load contract. “Steady welded line” in the clarification is sound for the stated static-pressure load-path point; it must not mean that all steady-flow resultant loads vanish. An interrupted axial path or untied bellows can create real unbalanced thrust. Bentley's older, vendor-authored explanation distinguishes pressure extension from untied-joint/transient thrust, but is supplementary conceptual corroboration, not a universal mechanics authority. [Bentley AutoPIPE KB0025326](https://bentleysystems.service-now.com/community?id=kb_article&sysparm_article=KB0025326).

## 5. Application envelope and return to the parent

The qualification applies to homogeneous isotropic, linear elastic material; circular uniform-bore straight segments; prescribed uniform pressure on reference geometry; small displacement/strain; and section-uniform thermal eigenstrain. Material selection/interpolation must yield a common justified `E,nu` pair before `G=E/[2(1+nu)]`. Finite `E>0` and `-1<nu<1/2` are mathematical admissibility bounds, not proof that an entered pair describes a real material.

The external-pressure reference needs particular care. For independently applied internal/external pressures, the transverse Lamé constant is `(pi Ai-po Ao)/As`, not merely `(pi-po)Ai/As`. Replacing both by differential pressure and silently setting the outside to zero omits the common hydrostatic part. The present contract is correct for its declared zero-external increment/reference and signed linear increments; it is not an exact arbitrary submerged/external-pressure state or a collapse assessment. No new external-pressure formula is activated here.

Unequal wall or material segments with equal bore preserve global cap cancellation, but each interface has local three-dimensional stress effects absent from the long-annulus model. End-cap restraint of radial expansion, weld/nozzle stress concentration, local thermal gradients, yielding, ovalization, contact, slender-member instability, prestress-dependent lateral response, fluid inertia, and large rotations remain outside this qualification. Superposition is valid only within the selected fixed-geometry linear approximation. The stiff, fully restrained thermal fixture is a useful algebraic state; it does not itself establish yield or stability adequacy for a real pipe.
