## Representation and operation obligations

1. `wide/multi.rs:18–45,394–461` represents a nonzero Wide16 as signed normalized 1024-bit integer times `2^(e−1023)`, with checked leading exponent in ±2^62; signed zero is separately represented. Its supported context precision is 2…1024. A fixed certificate precision must be explicitly selected; no endpoint can rely on unannounced precision escalation. At p=1024 all Wide16 operands fit TwoProduct’s input premise. At lower p each input must be checked or rounded outward first.
2. Wide16 arithmetic has no tiny subnormal/underflow lattice: a nonzero exact result either remains nonzero or refuses on exponent range. This is essential to the nonzero adjacent-step proof. Huge operand exponent gaps can still exceed exact-sum span and must refuse, not silently truncate.
3. `div_rounded:522–550` performs one initial compare/subtract and exactly 1025 restoring iterations at L=16, followed by one rounding with exact remainder sticky. Its normalized quotient has 1025 or 1026 significant bits. `sqrt_rounded:553–594` uses exactly 1026 two-radicand-bit iterations, with 17 active limbs in three 32-limb arrays; exponent parity chooses shift 1026 or 1027, retaining an exact residual. Neither procedure is Newton iteration or refinement.
4. `round_detail` keeps top p bits, exact round bit and all lower/sticky information; ties increment only odd retained integers, carry renormalizes and increments checked exponent. Products are full 32-limb schoolbook multiplication (256 limb products). TwoProduct returns s+e exactly only when operands fit p, and either component can refuse at an exponent boundary. A wrapper cannot assume an exact product survives merely because its rounded s survives.
5. Directed add/sub/mul in `directed.rs` form exact sums, round nearest once, compare exact−nearest and take at most one lattice-adjacent step when needed. Division requires b>0 and compares q*b−a; upward corrects a negative side, downward a positive side. For negative numerator the same sign argument holds because b>0.
6. A new directed sqrt can use q=RN_p(sqrt(a)), exact q*q−a, and one adjacent step: on q≥0, q*q−a has the sign of q−sqrt(a). Correct a negative side upward or positive side downward. Require a≥0 before invoking; a=0 returns exact zero. Do not epsilon-clamp a negative radicand. RN being between the bracketing neighbors proves one step sufficient; sample checks alone do not.
7. `directed.rs:26–55` uses magnitude unit `2^(e−p+1)` away from zero, also toward zero except at an exact power of two, where predecessor spacing is `2^(e−p)`. The sign of the unit follows direction/sign. Exact zero is never stepped. Exponent overflow and a step’s sum failure remain refusals.

## Exact sums, carries and fresh-local accounting

`ExactWideSum` owns two 128-u64 magnitudes (8192 bits each), admits term span ≤8128 bits and reserves 64 carry bits. The span is from lowest set bit to highest input-term leading bit, not the current net. Cancellation does not reset that history. A bounded wrapper needs an explicit inserted-term ceiling much less than 2^64; a carry-headroom claim based on span alone is insufficient for unbounded reuse.

`add_raw:218–295` can move both magnitudes on an anchor change, trim into 130 limbs, insert and propagate carry. Its carry loop is bounded by the physical 128-limb capacity under the headroom premise; it is not a constant-price single integer addition. `add_scaled` visits both signs of a source sum, and `add_product_of` has an outer loop over up to 128 limbs. Those generic routines must be excluded or explicitly priced by a fixed call grammar. An API accepting arbitrary-length integer slices also needs its own bound before scans/copies.

`clear:188–196` preserves work counters and zeroes only the used prefix. A refused add can leave partial state; `reset:198–210` clears the full magnitudes. A fresh accumulator/context per primitive, destroyed on success or failure, is an adequate safety design. Reuse requires full reset after failure and either bounded cumulative work or correctly checked deltas. Source `SumWork` fields use ordinary increments internally despite saturating totals/merges; wrapping prevention is established by a fresh bounded call, not by its aggregate accessor.

At L=16 existing context tariffs are add/sub/round=32, mul=256, div=17442, sqrt=18468, TwoProduct=320. These are source-accounted LME values only. Exact-sum raw inserts, shifts, nettings, rounds and failed work are additional. They do not cover all zeroing, copying, decoding, comparison, adapter, callback or facade work. A separate checked reservation can bound integer microsteps, but is not automatically an adopted kernel LME tariff or elapsed-time guarantee.

Fresh low-arity wrappers make all work finite: per raw insert, source work is bounded conservatively by ≤128 carried limbs, ≤256 shifted limbs and the explicit term limb count+1; each round nets ≤256, passes ≤128 limbs and charges one context round; sign comparison ≤128. Any proposal’s tighter price must enumerate actual successful and refused call paths, including conditional step and zero branches. No cumulative counter may wrap before admission testing.

## Closed comparisons and unchanged public predicates
